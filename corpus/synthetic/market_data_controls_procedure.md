# Meridian Bank Market Data Controls Procedure

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT — Meridian Bank is a fictional institution. This document was written for a portfolio project and is not the policy of any real firm.

**Document ID:** MDCP
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective date:** 2022-01-01
**Owner:** Head of Data Operations

## MDCP-1 Purpose and Scope

### MDCP-1.1 Purpose
This procedure governs the quality controls applied to the market data feeding Meridian Bank's pricing and risk calculations. It defines the checks Data Operations performs on incoming market data, the severity levels assigned to any finding, how a finding affects that day's revaluation, and the procedure for investigating and correcting a data issue once it is identified, including where a data issue coincides with a limit breach or warning under the Market Risk Limit Policy (MRLP). This procedure exists because the Bank's Value-at-Risk, stress, and limit-monitoring calculations are only as reliable as the market data feeding them; an undetected bad print can produce a spurious limit breach or, conversely, mask a real one, and this procedure is designed to catch both failure modes before they mislead Market Risk Management or a risk manager acting on an escalation note.

### MDCP-1.2 Scope
This procedure applies to the twelve market data series used in the Bank's daily risk process. Six series are sourced from Yahoo Finance (yfinance): SPY, AAPL, MSFT, and JPM closing prices, and the EURUSD=X and INR=X foreign exchange rates. Six series are sourced from the Federal Reserve Economic Data service (FRED): the DGS2, DGS5, and DGS10 Treasury constant-maturity yields, the VIXCLS implied-volatility index, and the DEXUSEU and DEXINUS foreign exchange rates, which serve as a second source for the FX cross-checks described in MDCP-2.2. This procedure covers the ingestion, validation, and severity treatment of these twelve series only; it does not cover the trade book itself, which is validated separately, or counterparty reference data. The twelve series divide into four calendar peer groups used by the missed-print check in MDCP-3.1: equity (SPY, AAPL, MSFT, JPM, VIXCLS), FX (EURUSD=X, INR=X), Treasury (DGS2, DGS5, DGS10), and FRED FX (DEXUSEU, DEXINUS). A newly introduced series must be added to this section and assigned a peer group before this procedure applies to it.

### MDCP-1.3 Synthetic-document status
Meridian Bank does not exist. This procedure was authored for a portfolio project and describes controls applied to publicly available market data series (from Yahoo Finance and FRED) for a synthetic trading book and a synthetic risk process. It is written in the register and structure of a real bank's data governance procedure, including realistic section identifiers, so that it can serve as a realistic retrieval corpus for the project's automated documentation-citation checks. No content in this document should be read as, or relied upon as, the data governance procedure of any real financial institution. The thresholds and parameters described here were derived from a defined historical training period of the same public data series and carry no meaning beyond this project.

## MDCP-2 Data Sources and Timing

### MDCP-2.1 Primary sources
The Bank's primary sources for the twelve series in scope are Yahoo Finance (yfinance) and FRED. Yahoo Finance supplies daily open-high-low-close data for SPY, AAPL, MSFT, and JPM, used for both the equity risk factors and, for SPY and the single-stock names, as volatility targets for option pricing, and closing rates for EURUSD=X and INR=X. FRED supplies the DGS2, DGS5, and DGS10 Treasury constant-maturity yields used to build the Bank's USD discount curve, the VIXCLS index used as the implied-volatility proxy for SPY options, and DEXUSEU and DEXINUS, which are not used directly in pricing but serve exclusively as a second, independent source for the FX cross-checks in MDCP-2.2 and MDCP-3.3. Daily history is maintained from 2015-01-01 to the latest available date for all twelve series.

### MDCP-2.2 Second sources for FX cross-checks
DEXUSEU and DEXINUS from FRED serve as the second source against which the Bank's primary FX rates, EURUSD=X and INR=X from Yahoo Finance, are cross-checked. DEXUSEU quotes US dollars per euro, the same convention as EURUSD=X, and DEXINUS quotes Indian rupees per US dollar, the same convention as INR=X, so the two sources can be compared directly without a unit conversion. Because the Yahoo Finance close for a given date reflects that day's trading, while the FRED series reports a noon New York print dated to the previous business day's fixing convention, the cross-source comparison in MDCP-3.3 compares a given date's Yahoo Finance close against the FRED print carried forward from the previous business day, not against the same calendar date's FRED print.

### MDCP-2.3 Business-day panel, no forward fill
All twelve series are aligned onto a common business-day panel before any control is applied. Series are not forward-filled to cover a missing print: if a series does not have a print for a given business day for which its calendar peers, as defined in MDCP-3.1, do print, that absence is treated as a missed print and evaluated under the calendar-based check in MDCP-3.1, not silently carried forward from the prior day's value. This is a deliberate design choice: forward filling a missing print would mask a genuine data gap and could produce an artificially stale-looking series that would then also need to be caught by the staleness check in MDCP-3.2, so the panel is built without forward fill and gaps are surfaced directly.

## MDCP-3 Controls

### MDCP-3.1 Hard rule checks
Data Operations applies the following rule-based checks to every business day's print for each series: prices must be positive; a series is checked against its calendar peer group (equity: SPY, AAPL, MSFT, JPM, VIXCLS; FX: EURUSD=X, INR=X; Treasury: DGS2, DGS5, DGS10; FRED FX: DEXUSEU, DEXINUS) for a missed print, meaning a null value on a day when most of its peer group did print and the day is not a holiday; yields (DGS2, DGS5, DGS10) must fall within −1% to 20%; and each series may not move by more than a configured maximum absolute 1-day move, set at roughly twice the largest move observed in that asset class over 2015–2021. The configured maximum moves are: 0.35 (log return) for SPY, AAPL, MSFT, and JPM; 0.06 (log return) for EURUSD=X, INR=X, DEXUSEU, and DEXINUS; 60 basis points (absolute change) for DGS2, DGS5, and DGS10; and 1.6 (log change) for VIXCLS. A violation of any of these rule checks is a critical finding, per MDCP-4.1.

### MDCP-3.2 Staleness
A series is flagged for staleness when it prints three or more consecutive identical values on business days. This check applies only to the liquid series expected to move most business days: SPY, AAPL, MSFT, JPM, EURUSD=X, INR=X, and VIXCLS. The three Treasury yield series (DGS2, DGS5, DGS10) are excluded from the staleness check, because Treasury yields are quoted to one basis point and can genuinely remain unchanged for several consecutive business days during quiet periods without indicating a data feed problem; applying the staleness check to Treasuries would generate a high rate of false findings on entirely legitimate prints. A staleness finding is a critical finding, per MDCP-4.1, on the affected liquid series. A staleness finding does not by itself indicate whether the underlying price genuinely did not move or the feed stopped updating; the investigation steps in MDCP-5.2, particularly the vendor history check, resolve which is the case.

### MDCP-3.3 Cross-source comparison
EURUSD=X is compared against DEXUSEU, and INR=X is compared against DEXINUS, using the previous-business-day alignment described in MDCP-2.2. A finding is raised when the two sources differ by more than 200 basis points, a tolerance set just above the largest gap observed between the two source pairs over 2015–2021. This check exists because a bad print in the Bank's primary FX source will generally not be replicated in the independent FRED series, so a material divergence between the two is strong evidence of a data quality problem in one of them, most likely the primary source feeding the Bank's pricing. A cross-source finding is a critical finding, per MDCP-4.1, on the affected FX series. The 200 basis point tolerance is set above ordinary intraday-to-noon timing noise between the two sources, so a finding indicates a divergence beyond what the alignment convention in MDCP-2.2 alone would explain.

### MDCP-3.4 Anomaly model
In addition to the rule-based checks in MDCP-3.1 through MDCP-3.3, Data Operations runs an Isolation Forest anomaly detection model over each series, using features that include the return z-score, next-day reversal, a rolling volatility ratio comparing a short window to a longer trailing window, and, for the FX series, the cross-source difference. The model is trained once on data through 2021-12-31 and is not retrained on the 2022 onward evaluation period, so that its behavior on live data reflects genuine out-of-sample detection rather than a model that has seen the outcomes it is being asked to flag. A print scoring above the cutoff set from the training data's score distribution is flagged. Unlike the rule-based checks in MDCP-3.1 through MDCP-3.3, an Isolation Forest flag is a warning finding, not a critical one, per MDCP-4.2, reflecting the model's statistical rather than deterministic basis.

## MDCP-4 Severity and Treatment

### MDCP-4.1 Critical findings
A finding from any of the rule-based checks — the hard rule checks in MDCP-3.1, the staleness check in MDCP-3.2, or the cross-source comparison in MDCP-3.3 — is classified as critical. When a critical finding is raised on a series for a given business day, that series' value is held flat in that day's revaluation: the prior day's value is used in place of the flagged print for pricing and risk purposes, rather than passing the suspect print through to the Bank's calculations. This treatment prioritizes not contaminating the risk calculation with a value the rule-based checks have deterministically identified as implausible, even though holding a factor flat is itself an approximation that can distort the resulting risk figures, as described in MDCP-4.3.

### MDCP-4.2 Warning findings
A finding from the Isolation Forest anomaly model, described in MDCP-3.4, is classified as a warning. Unlike a critical finding, a warning does not cause the affected factor to be held flat: the series remains live and its actual print flows through to that day's pricing and risk calculation. A warning finding is reviewed by Data Operations before the next business day's run, following the investigation steps in MDCP-5.2, but does not automatically alter that day's already-completed calculation. This treatment reflects that the anomaly model's flags are statistical signals rather than deterministic rule violations, and holding a factor flat on a warning alone would risk suppressing genuine, if unusual, market moves that the rule-based checks did not independently flag. Because a warning finding leaves the print live, any limit breach or warning-band observation on the same day that traces to the flagged factor must still be evaluated under the routing procedure in MDCP-5, exactly as for a critical finding, rather than being dismissed as a lesser concern.

### MDCP-4.3 Consequences
Both severity treatments carry a consequence for the reliability of downstream risk figures, and Market Risk Management must be aware of both when interpreting a Value-at-Risk or stress result. Holding a factor flat in response to a critical finding understates that factor's contribution to Value-at-Risk on the affected day, since the actual, possibly extreme, market move is replaced with no move at all. Conversely, allowing a factor to remain live in response to a warning finding means that a genuinely bad print, if the anomaly model is the only check to catch it, can flow directly into the Bank's Value-at-Risk and stress calculations and inflate them, potentially causing a limit breach that reflects a data error rather than real market risk. This is precisely the scenario the root-cause classification in MRLP-5.3 and the routing procedure in MDCP-5 are designed to catch and correct.

## MDCP-5 Data-Issue Routing Procedure

### MDCP-5.1 Trigger
The routing procedure in this section is triggered whenever a limit breach or a warning-band observation, as defined in MRLP-4, coincides with a data-quality finding — critical or warning — on a risk factor that materially contributes to the breached or elevated metric. When this trigger condition is met, the case is routed to Data Operations using the action code `route_to_data_ops`, and it is explicitly not treated as a limit escalation under MRLP-6.1 through MRLP-6.3 while the data investigation is pending. This distinction matters because escalating a data-driven anomaly through the limit-breach paths would direct a desk head's or the CRO's attention to a position or market event that may not be real, rather than to the data issue that actually needs correcting.

### MDCP-5.2 Data Operations investigation steps
Once a case is routed under MDCP-5.1, Data Operations investigates the flagged print through the following steps: comparing the primary source's value against any available secondary or cross-source value for the same series and date; checking the vendor's own revision or correction history for that print, since vendors sometimes correct erroneous prints after initial publication; checking whether the value reverses on the next business day, which is often a strong signal of a transient data error rather than a genuine, persistent market move; and, based on this evidence, either confirming the print as genuine or correcting it to a verified value. These steps are performed regardless of whether the original finding was critical or warning severity, since both can turn out to be either a genuine error or a real, if unusual, market move.

### MDCP-5.3 Correction and rerun
Where Data Operations corrects a print, the corrected value replaces the flagged value in the market data panel, and Market Risk Management reruns the affected day's pricing and risk calculations using the corrected data. Once the rerun is complete, the limit status of every affected metric is re-assessed under MRLP-4, using the newly computed values, not the original values that triggered the routing in MDCP-5.1. A metric that showed a breach or warning under the uncorrected data may show a different status, including `ok`, once rerun with the corrected print, and it is this re-assessed status that governs any subsequent escalation decision, not the original, uncorrected one. The rerun uses the same daily-run process described in MRLP-4.1 and produces new result identifiers for the affected metrics; any escalation note prepared after a rerun must cite these new identifiers rather than the pre-correction figures, consistent with the evidence standard in MRLP-7.2.

### MDCP-5.4 Timelines
Data Operations investigates and resolves a routed case on the same business day it is identified wherever possible, given that limit status and any pending escalation decision depend on the outcome of the investigation. Where a case cannot be fully resolved the same day — for example, because a vendor correction is still pending — Data Operations records the case as open and communicates the expected resolution timeline to Market Risk Management, so that the affected metric's status is understood to be provisional until the investigation concludes and, if needed, a rerun under MDCP-5.3 is performed. An open case does not suspend the Bank's daily risk run for other, unaffected metrics; only the specific metric or metrics tied to the flagged factor are treated as provisional, and Market Risk Management continues ordinary monitoring under MRLP-4 for everything else in the meantime.

### MDCP-5.5 Confirmed-genuine handoff
Where Data Operations concludes that a flagged print is genuine rather than erroneous — for example, because it is corroborated by the cross-source comparison, is not reversed the next day, and matches the vendor's confirmed history — the case is handed back to Market Risk Management. Market Risk Management then classifies the underlying breach, if one is still present after this confirmation, as a market move under MRLP-5.2, rather than as bad market data under MRLP-5.3, since the evidence now indicates the print reflects a real market event rather than a data quality issue. This handoff closes Data Operations' role in the case and returns it to the standard escalation paths in MRLP-6. The confirmation evidence gathered under MDCP-5.2 should still be referenced in the resulting escalation note, since it demonstrates that the market-move classification was reached only after a data-quality concern was actively investigated and ruled out, not assumed by default.

## MDCP-6 Records and Reporting

### MDCP-6.1 Finding log
Every finding from the controls described in MDCP-3, whether critical or warning, is written to the `dq_findings` record, capturing the date, the affected factor, the specific check that produced the finding, its severity, and a detail field describing the finding, such as the observed value and the threshold it violated. This log is the authoritative source for the data-quality evidence cited in an escalation note's root-cause classification under MRLP-5.3, and for the routing decision under MDCP-5.1. Findings are logged regardless of whether they are later confirmed as genuine market moves under MDCP-5.5; the log reflects what the controls detected at the time, with the outcome of any subsequent investigation recorded alongside it rather than overwriting the original finding. The finding log is the record Market Risk Management queries to validate a bad-market-data root cause under MRLP-5.3, and any finding cited in an escalation note must correspond to an entry here, not an informal report.

### MDCP-6.2 Monthly control reporting
Data Operations produces a monthly report summarizing control performance: the count of findings by series, check, and severity; the number of cases routed to Data Operations under MDCP-5.1 and their resolution outcomes, including how many were corrected versus confirmed genuine; and any patterns suggesting a control threshold, such as the maximum move or cross-source tolerance in MDCP-3, may need review. This report is provided to Market Risk Management and informs, but does not replace, the annual limit calibration review described in MRLP-3.5, since a change in the frequency or nature of data-quality findings can affect the reliability of the historical data used for that calibration. The monthly report also tracks the same-business-day resolution timeline required under MDCP-5.4, flagging any case that remained open beyond the expected timeline, so that recurring delays in a particular series or check can be identified and addressed rather than treated as isolated incidents.
