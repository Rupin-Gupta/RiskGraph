# Meridian Bank Market Risk Limit Policy

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT — Meridian Bank is a fictional institution. This document was written for a portfolio project and is not the policy of any real firm.

**Document ID:** MRLP
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective date:** 2022-01-01
**Owner:** Head of Market Risk Management

## MRLP-1 Purpose, Scope and Status

### MRLP-1.1 Purpose
This policy sets out how Meridian Bank (the "Bank") sets, monitors, and enforces market risk limits on its trading book. It defines the limit hierarchy, the metrics used to measure utilization, the thresholds that separate normal operation from a limit breach, and the process by which a breach is investigated, classified by root cause, and escalated. It also sets the standards that an escalation note must meet before it is sent, and the approval steps required before any note leaves the Bank's internal systems. The policy is binding on Market Risk Management, desk heads, Data Operations, and risk managers on duty. It must be read together with the Market Data Controls Procedure (MDCP), which governs the quality of the market data feeding the risk calculations described here, and with the Bank's daily risk-run documentation, which describes how the metrics in this policy are produced.

### MRLP-1.2 Scope
This policy applies to the market risk of Meridian Bank's trading book, which spans three desks: foreign exchange (fx), interest rate products (rates), and equity derivatives (equity_derivatives). It applies at both the desk level and the firm level, where the firm level aggregates exposure across all three desks. The book against which this policy is enforced is a synthetic portfolio of approximately 46 trades booked against eight fictional counterparties (CP01 through CP08), including FX forwards and spot, USD fixed-float interest rate swaps and Treasury positions, and European equity options with cash equity hedges. This policy covers market risk limits (Value-at-Risk and stress loss) and references counterparty credit risk limits (potential future exposure) only as a placeholder pending their calibration. It does not cover operational risk, liquidity risk, or capital adequacy, which are governed by separate policies outside this document's scope.

### MRLP-1.3 Synthetic-document status
Meridian Bank does not exist. This policy, along with the Bank's book, counterparties, limits, and every figure quoted in it, is synthetic and was authored for a portfolio project demonstrating an agentic risk-monitoring system. It is written in the register and structure of a real bank policy so that it can be used as a realistic retrieval corpus for that system, including realistic section identifiers that the system's citation checks can validate. No content in this document should be read as, or relied upon as, the market risk policy of any real financial institution. Numeric limits, thresholds, and calibration figures are illustrative and derived from a synthetic trading book and a synthetic history of market data; they carry no regulatory or commercial meaning outside this project.

### MRLP-1.4 Relationship to the Basel Framework
This policy operationalizes, at Meridian Bank, the market risk principles set out in the Basel Framework's market risk standard (the "MAR" chapters, including the boundary and standardized/internal-model provisions in the MAR30–MAR33 range) and, for counterparty credit risk exposure measures referenced in MRLP-3.4, the counterparty credit risk chapters (CRE50–CRE55). Where the Basel Framework sets minimum standards for model validation, backtesting, and capital treatment, this policy sets the Bank's internal limit framework, which is generally more conservative and more granular than the regulatory minimum, and is designed to trigger management action well before any regulatory threshold is reached. Daily backtesting of the Bank's Value-at-Risk model against MAR32's backtesting expectations is described in MRLP-10. This policy does not restate Basel paragraph text; escalation notes that cite the Basel Framework must reference the relevant chapter and paragraph directly from the regulatory corpus, not from this policy.

## MRLP-2 Roles and Responsibilities

### MRLP-2.1 Board Risk Committee
The Board Risk Committee has ultimate oversight of the Bank's market risk appetite and this policy. It approves the firm-level Value-at-Risk limit and the firm-level stress-loss limit annually, or more frequently if market conditions or the Bank's business mix change materially. It receives notification of any firm-level Value-at-Risk breach and any stress-loss limit breach, and it reviews the annual limit calibration described in MRLP-3.5. The Committee does not participate in day-to-day limit monitoring or in the classification of individual breaches; that operational responsibility sits with Market Risk Management. The Committee's role is to satisfy itself, at a governance level, that the limit framework remains appropriate to the Bank's risk appetite and that escalation and remediation processes are functioning as designed. It reviews a summary of breach frequency and root-cause distribution at each scheduled meeting.

### MRLP-2.2 Chief Risk Officer (CRO)
The Chief Risk Officer owns the Bank's overall risk appetite and is the primary point of escalation for firm-level Value-at-Risk breaches and stress-loss limit breaches. The CRO is notified the same business day as any such breach, per MRLP-6.2 and MRLP-6.3, and is responsible for deciding whether a breach warrants Board Risk Committee notification, additional risk mitigation, or a temporary limit increase under MRLP-9. The CRO delegates day-to-day limit monitoring and desk-level breach handling to Market Risk Management but retains authority over any exception to this policy. The CRO also approves changes to the risk model that could affect limit calibration, in coordination with the model owners, and signs off on the annual review of this policy described in MRLP-11.

### MRLP-2.3 Market Risk Management
Market Risk Management (MRM) is the independent risk control unit responsible for this policy. MRM owns the daily limit monitoring process: it runs, or oversees the automated running of, the daily Value-at-Risk and stress calculations, computes utilization against each limit, and determines the status of each metric. MRM performs the initial root-cause classification of any breach or warning-band observation under MRLP-5, drafts or reviews escalation notes under MRLP-7, and routes suspected data-quality issues to Data Operations under MRLP-6.6. MRM is independent of the trading desks and reports through the CRO, not through any desk head, to preserve the independence of limit monitoring and breach classification. MRM also maintains the record of limit statuses, breaches, and escalations required under MRLP-4.5, and leads the annual limit calibration exercise under MRLP-3.5.

### MRLP-2.4 Desk heads
Each desk head (fx, rates, equity_derivatives) is responsible for managing positions within that desk's Value-at-Risk limit and for responding promptly when MRM notifies them of a desk-level breach. Desk heads must be able to explain, on request, the composition and rationale of new positions that Market Risk Management identifies as contributing to a breach, and must cooperate with any pre-trade check review triggered under MRLP-6.4. Where a breach is classified as a position change, the desk head is responsible for proposing remediation, whether that is reducing the position, hedging it, or requesting a temporary limit increase under MRLP-9. Desk heads do not have authority to override a limit status or reclassify a breach; that authority sits with Market Risk Management and, for exceptions, with the CRO.

### MRLP-2.5 Data Operations
Data Operations owns the quality of the market data that feeds the Bank's pricing and risk calculations, under the Market Data Controls Procedure (MDCP). Data Operations is responsible for investigating any data-quality finding that is linked to a limit breach or warning under MRLP-6.6 and MDCP-5, comparing data sources, checking vendor history, determining whether a suspect print reverses the next day, and confirming or correcting the affected series. Data Operations does not classify limit breaches or draft escalation notes; its role is confined to the data investigation and correction described in MDCP-5, after which it hands the corrected data back to Market Risk Management for a rerun of the affected metrics. Data Operations also maintains the data-quality finding log described in MDCP-6 and reports monthly on control performance.

### MRLP-2.6 Risk managers on duty
A risk manager on duty is designated each business day to review escalation notes drafted by Market Risk Management before they are sent, and to make the manual determination required whenever a breach is classified as unknown or of insufficient evidence under MRLP-5.5. The risk manager on duty is the human-in-the-loop approver referenced throughout this policy: no escalation note is dispatched without that approver's explicit decision, recorded as described in MRLP-8. The risk manager on duty may edit a note before approving it, request additional evidence before deciding, or reject a note outright with a documented reason. This role rotates among qualified members of Market Risk Management and is distinct from the desk-level or CRO-level notification recipients described in MRLP-6.

## MRLP-3 Limit Framework

### MRLP-3.1 Limit hierarchy
Meridian Bank's market risk limits are set at two levels: firm and desk. The firm-level limit constrains the Bank's aggregate market risk across all three desks (fx, rates, equity_derivatives) and is the more binding constraint from a governance perspective, since a firm-level breach is escalated to the Chief Risk Officer regardless of which desk contributes most to it. Desk-level limits constrain each desk independently and are designed so that, under normal diversification, full utilization of all three desk limits simultaneously would not by itself breach the firm limit. Limits are expressed in two metrics: 1-day 99% historical-simulation Value-at-Risk (desk and firm) and worst-case stress loss (firm only, for now). A third metric, counterparty potential future exposure, is anticipated at the counterparty level but is not yet calibrated; see MRLP-3.4. All limits are denominated in US dollars, the Bank's reporting currency.

### MRLP-3.2 VaR limits
Value-at-Risk limits are expressed as 1-day 99% historical-simulation Value-at-Risk, computed over a rolling 500-day lookback window of historical market moves applied to the current book. Utilization is computed daily as described in MRLP-4.1. The current limits, calibrated per MRLP-3.5, are:

| Scope | Metric | Limit (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Firm | 1-day 99% VaR | 3,060,000 |
| FX desk | 1-day 99% VaR | 2,180,000 |
| Rates desk | 1-day 99% VaR | 1,630,000 |
| Equity derivatives desk | 1-day 99% VaR | 1,300,000 |

These limits apply every business day the Bank runs its risk process. A desk's limit is independent of the firm limit: a desk can be within its own limit while the firm is in breach, or vice versa, depending on diversification and correlation across desks on that day. Any change to these limits requires the annual review and calibration process in MRLP-3.5, or an approved temporary increase under MRLP-9.

### MRLP-3.3 Stress-loss limit
In addition to Value-at-Risk, the Bank sets a firm-level stress-loss limit of USD 25,300,000. This limit constrains the worst loss the current book would suffer under any of the Bank's configured stress scenarios, evaluated by full revaluation. The configured scenarios are two historical scenarios — the worst single day of the March 2020 crash window, and the day of the largest 1-day Treasury curve move in 2022 — and three hypothetical scenarios: a parallel 200 basis point upward shift of the Treasury curve, a 10% appreciation of the US dollar against the Indian rupee, and a combined equity crash of a 20% decline in SPY, AAPL, MSFT, and JPM alongside a 50% rise in the VIX. The stress-loss metric reported against the limit is the single worst loss across all five scenarios, not an average or a sum. There is currently no desk-level stress-loss limit; stress loss is monitored only at the firm level.

### MRLP-3.4 Counterparty PFE limits
Counterparty potential future exposure (PFE) limits are anticipated for each of the Bank's eight counterparties (CP01 through CP08) but have not yet been set; the relevant configuration currently holds a null value for every counterparty. Until PFE limits are calibrated and approved by the Board Risk Committee, counterparty exposure is monitored informationally by Market Risk Management but is not subject to the breach, status, or escalation mechanics described in MRLP-4 through MRLP-7. This section is a placeholder recording the Bank's intent to extend the limit framework to counterparty credit risk exposure measures, consistent with the Basel Framework's counterparty credit risk chapters (CRE50–CRE55), once the underlying exposure engine and calibration data are in place. No escalation note may cite a PFE breach until this section is updated with approved limits.

### MRLP-3.5 Limit calibration and annual review
Value-at-Risk and stress-loss limits are calibrated using the 98th percentile of each metric's daily history over the calibration window from 2022-01-01 to 2025-12-31, rounded to three significant figures. This calibration is designed so that breaches are rare — occurring in roughly the tail 2% of observed days — but do occur in the historical record rather than being set so conservatively that a breach is never triggered by real data. The calibration is performed and documented by Market Risk Management using the Bank's backtesting tooling, and the resulting limits, along with the number of historical breach days implied, are reviewed and approved by the Board Risk Committee at least annually. An out-of-cycle recalibration may be triggered by a material change to the book's composition, a change to the risk model described in MRLP-10.3, or a Board Risk Committee decision to revise the Bank's risk appetite.

## MRLP-4 Limit Monitoring and Status

### MRLP-4.1 Daily run and utilization
Market Risk Management runs the Bank's risk process every business day, computing Value-at-Risk and stress loss at the desk and firm level from that day's trade book and market data. For every metric and scope with a defined limit, utilization is computed as utilization = metric value / limit. Utilization is expressed as a percentage and is the single figure used to determine status under MRLP-4.2. The daily run also produces the underlying result records — the specific Value-at-Risk figure, the limit it is compared against, and the resulting utilization and status — which are persisted and referenced by a result identifier. Any figure quoted in an escalation note must trace back to one of these result identifiers, per the evidence standard in MRLP-7.2; utilization is never estimated or recalculated by hand outside the daily run.

### MRLP-4.2 Status thresholds
Each metric-scope combination is assigned one of three statuses based on its utilization for that day: `ok` when utilization is below 90%, `warning` when utilization is at or above 90% but below 100%, and `breach` when utilization is at or above 100%. These thresholds are fixed across all desks and at the firm level; there is no per-desk variation in the warning or breach threshold. Status is computed independently for every metric-scope pair on the daily run; a firm-level Value-at-Risk breach does not automatically imply a desk-level breach, and a single desk being in breach does not automatically imply the firm is in breach. The status recorded for a given day is final for that day's run and is what all downstream classification, escalation, and reporting must reference.

### MRLP-4.3 Warning band and near misses
Utilization between 90% and 99.99% inclusive is the warning band. A metric in the warning band is not a breach and must not be escalated as one. No action beyond ordinary monitoring is required when a metric enters the warning band: Market Risk Management records the observation as part of its daily record keeping under MRLP-4.5 so that a pattern of persistent or worsening warning-band utilization can be identified over time, but no notification is sent to a desk head, the CRO, or the Board Risk Committee solely because a metric is in the warning band. Treating a warning-band observation as a breach, or escalating it, is itself a process error that this policy is designed to prevent; see the alert validation requirement in MRLP-4.4.

### MRLP-4.4 Alert validation
Before any action is taken on an alert concerning a possible limit issue, the alert must be validated against the official limit status produced by that day's daily run, as described in MRLP-4.1 and MRLP-4.2. An alert is not a true breach, and requires no escalation action, unless the corresponding metric's utilization is at or above 100% in the official run for that date. This validation step exists because alerts can originate from monitoring systems, ad hoc queries, or manual observation that may be based on stale, partial, or recalculated figures rather than the official daily run. Any investigation, root-cause classification, or escalation note must reference the status from the official run, identified by its result identifier, and must not proceed on the basis of an unvalidated alert. An alert that fails this validation is closed with the root cause `no_true_breach` under MRLP-5.4.

### MRLP-4.5 Record keeping
Market Risk Management maintains a daily record of limit status for every metric-scope combination, including the underlying metric value, the applicable limit, computed utilization, and resulting status. This record includes warning-band observations as well as breaches, so that near misses are visible for trend analysis even though they do not trigger escalation. The record also captures, for every breach, its eventual root-cause classification, the escalation path taken, and the outcome of the approval step described in MRLP-8. This record is the authoritative source for the annual limit calibration review under MRLP-3.5, for backtesting exception counts under MRLP-10, and for any audit or regulatory inquiry into the Bank's limit monitoring process. Records are retained in the Bank's risk systems and are not to be recreated from memory or informal notes.

## MRLP-5 Breach Root-Cause Classification

### MRLP-5.1 Position change
A breach is classified as a position change when the Value-at-Risk explain decomposition (position effect, market effect, interaction) attributes a materially positive position effect to the day-over-day change in the breached metric, and the underlying positions have in fact changed — through new trades, amendments, or unwinds — since the prior run. The required evidence for this classification is the position-effect figure from the Value-at-Risk explain output, together with identification of the specific new or amended trades driving it, by trade identifier, desk, and instrument type. A position change classification should not be made solely because new trades exist on the book that day; the position effect must be shown to be a material driver of the breach, not merely coincident with unrelated trading activity. Where both a position change and a market move contribute materially, the classification should identify both contributions rather than forcing a single cause.

### MRLP-5.2 Market move
A breach is classified as a market move when the book's positions are materially unchanged from the prior run and the Value-at-Risk explain decomposition attributes the change in the breached metric to the market effect — that is, to a shift in observed market moves or the prevailing volatility regime feeding the historical simulation, rather than to new or amended trades. The required evidence is the market-effect figure from the Value-at-Risk explain output and confirmation that no data-quality finding exists on the risk factors materially contributing to the breach. This second condition distinguishes a genuine market move from a data artifact: if a risk factor contributing to the breach also carries a data-quality finding, the breach must instead be evaluated under MRLP-5.3, since an artificial market move looks identical to a real one in the explain decomposition unless the data-quality evidence is checked.

### MRLP-5.3 Bad market data
A breach is classified as bad market data when there is a data-quality finding — of critical or warning severity, as defined in the Market Data Controls Procedure (MDCP-4) — on a risk factor that materially contributes to the breached metric. Evidence for this classification includes the specific finding from the `dq_findings` record (factor, check, severity, and detail), and, where available, corroborating evidence such as a cross-source difference against the second source used for FX cross-checks, or a warning from the anomaly detection model. A critical finding means the affected factor was held flat in that day's revaluation, which can understate as well as overstate a metric; a warning finding means the factor remained live and could have flowed a bad print directly into the calculation. Either type of finding, on a contributing factor, is sufficient evidence for this classification; it is not necessary for the finding to be critical.

### MRLP-5.4 No true breach
A breach is classified as no true breach when the metric's utilization from the official daily run, as validated under MRLP-4.4, is in fact below 100%. This covers two situations: a near miss in the warning band (90% to 99.99% utilization, see MRLP-4.3), which was never a breach and requires no escalation by definition, and a false alert that failed the validation check in MRLP-4.4 against a status showing utilization below 100%. This classification exists precisely to prevent unnecessary escalation and to correctly close out alerts that do not correspond to an actual limit breach in the official record. No evidence beyond the validated status record is required for this classification; if the official run shows utilization below 100%, no further root-cause investigation is needed.

### MRLP-5.5 Unknown or insufficient evidence
A breach is classified as unknown when the available evidence does not clearly support any of the classifications in MRLP-5.1 through MRLP-5.4 — for example, when the Value-at-Risk explain decomposition shows a mixed or ambiguous attribution, when position and market effects are both small relative to the interaction term, or when a data-quality finding exists but does not clearly implicate a contributing factor. Rather than forcing an unsupported classification, this outcome is escalated to a risk manager on duty for manual review, per MRLP-6, along with all the evidence gathered so far. The risk manager on duty may request additional analysis, consult the desk head or Data Operations informally, or make a judgment call documented in the escalation note. This classification should be used sparingly; it is a safety valve for genuinely ambiguous cases, not a substitute for completing the analysis required by the other four categories.

## MRLP-6 Escalation Paths

### MRLP-6.1 Desk VaR limit breach
When a desk-level Value-at-Risk limit breach is validated under MRLP-4.4, Market Risk Management must notify the relevant desk head and the head of market risk on the same business day the breach is identified. This notification precedes, and is separate from, the fuller escalation note described in MRLP-7, which is prepared once the root-cause classification under MRLP-5 is complete. The purpose of the same-day notification is to ensure the desk is aware of the breach immediately, even before root cause is fully established, so that the desk head can begin considering remediation options described in MRLP-6.4 without waiting for the complete investigation. A desk-level breach that also causes a firm-level breach triggers both this notification and the firm-level notification in MRLP-6.2.

### MRLP-6.2 Firm VaR limit breach
When the firm-level Value-at-Risk limit breach is validated under MRLP-4.4, Market Risk Management must notify the Chief Risk Officer on the same business day. A firm-level breach is treated as more significant than a desk-level breach alone because it reflects the Bank's aggregate risk position rather than a single desk's contribution, and it is the CRO, not a desk head, who holds authority over firm-wide risk mitigation and any temporary limit increase under MRLP-9. The CRO notification includes the preliminary breach details available at the time — scope, metric, value, limit, and utilization — with the full root-cause classification and evidence to follow in the escalation note once available, consistent with the evidence standards in MRLP-7.2. Where a firm-level breach coincides with desk-level breaches, a single notification to the CRO should reference all contributing desks. The CRO may indicate, ahead of the completed escalation note, whether Board Risk Committee notification under MRLP-6.3 or an interim risk-mitigation instruction is warranted.

### MRLP-6.3 Stress-loss limit breach
A firm-level stress-loss limit breach is escalated to both the Chief Risk Officer and the Board Risk Committee. This reflects the more severe and less frequent nature of stress-loss breaches relative to Value-at-Risk breaches: a stress-loss breach indicates that the current book would suffer a loss exceeding the Bank's tolerance under at least one of the configured historical or hypothetical scenarios, which is a tail outcome the Board has a direct governance interest in. As with firm-level Value-at-Risk breaches, the initial notification may precede the completed root-cause classification, but the escalation note prepared under MRLP-7 must still include the full classification, evidence, and recommended action before the note is finalized for approval under MRLP-8. Because the stress-loss metric reflects the worst outcome across the five scenarios in MRLP-3.3, the notification and escalation note should identify which scenario produced the worst loss, since remediation or a temporary increase request under MRLP-9 will typically be scenario-specific.

### MRLP-6.4 Breach caused by a new position
When a breach is classified as a position change under MRLP-5.1, it is escalated to a risk manager on duty for review, in addition to the desk-level or firm-level notification already made under MRLP-6.1 or MRLP-6.2. The risk manager's review includes checking whether the new or amended trades identified as the cause passed the Bank's pre-trade limit checks correctly, and if not, why they were still booked. Remediation options for the desk head to consider, in consultation with the risk manager, are: reducing the position that drove the breach, hedging it to bring utilization back under 100%, or requesting a temporary limit increase under MRLP-9 if the position is intended to be held. This policy does not mandate any single remediation; the appropriate choice depends on the trading rationale for the position and the Bank's risk appetite at the time.

### MRLP-6.5 Market-driven breach
When a breach is classified as a market move under MRLP-5.2, it is escalated to a risk manager on duty for review, in addition to the desk-level or firm-level notification already made. Because the breach was not caused by the Bank's own trading decisions, this policy does not require, and generally discourages, a forced unwind of existing positions purely in reaction to a market-driven breach. Instead, the response is enhanced monitoring: Market Risk Management tracks the affected metric more closely on subsequent daily runs to determine whether the elevated utilization persists, worsens, or reverts as market conditions evolve. If the elevated utilization proves persistent, the desk head and risk manager on duty may still consider the remediation options described in MRLP-6.4, even though the original cause was market-driven rather than a new position.

### MRLP-6.6 Breach linked to suspected bad market data
When a breach or warning is classified, or suspected, as bad market data under MRLP-5.3, it must not be escalated through the limit-breach paths in MRLP-6.1 through MRLP-6.3. Instead, it is routed to Data Operations under MDCP-5 for investigation. This routing reflects the fact that a data-quality issue is not a market risk event requiring desk or CRO action; it is a data integrity issue requiring correction. Once Data Operations completes its investigation and either corrects the affected print or confirms it as genuine, the affected metric is rerun and its limit status is re-assessed under MRLP-4. If the print is confirmed genuine rather than erroneous, the case is handed back to Market Risk Management for classification as a market move under MRLP-5.2, and only then does it re-enter the escalation paths above if a breach persists after the rerun.

### MRLP-6.7 Summary routing table
The following table summarizes the escalation action for each root-cause classification, using the action codes recognized by the Bank's incident-handling system:

| Root cause | Action |
|---|---|
| `position_change` | `escalate_to_risk_manager` |
| `market_move` | `escalate_to_risk_manager` |
| `bad_market_data` | `route_to_data_ops` |
| `no_true_breach` | `no_action` |
| `unknown` | `escalate_to_risk_manager` |

This table governs the recommended action field of any escalation note or incident record. A note that recommends an action inconsistent with this table, given its stated root cause, does not meet the requirements of MRLP-7 and should not be approved for dispatch under MRLP-8. The desk-level and firm-level notifications described in MRLP-6.1 through MRLP-6.3 are separate, same-day communications that occur regardless of eventual root cause, and are not themselves the "action" recorded in this table.

## MRLP-7 Escalation Note Requirements

### MRLP-7.1 Required contents
Every escalation note must contain, at minimum: the identity of the limit breached (scope and metric), the value of the metric, the limit amount it is compared against, the resulting utilization, the root-cause classification determined under MRLP-5, the evidence supporting that classification, the recommended action consistent with the routing table in MRLP-6.7, and the policy and, where applicable, regulatory citations supporting the analysis. A note missing any of these elements is incomplete and must not be submitted for approval under MRLP-8. Where a breach has multiple contributing causes, as noted in MRLP-5.1, the note must present all material contributions rather than collapsing them into a single cause for the sake of brevity. The note's structure should make each of these elements easy for the approving risk manager to locate and verify independently.

### MRLP-7.2 Evidence standards
Every figure quoted in an escalation note must be traceable to a specific risk-system output, identified by its result identifier, and must not be an estimated, rounded from memory, or recalled figure. This applies to the breach metric itself, the limit, utilization, the Value-at-Risk explain decomposition figures used for root-cause classification, and any data-quality finding cited as evidence. Where a note references a specific trade as the cause of a position-change breach, it must identify the trade by its trade identifier rather than by description alone. This evidence standard exists so that every claim in a note can be independently re-verified against the underlying system of record, which is also the basis of the deterministic evidence check performed before a note is approved.

### MRLP-7.3 Citation requirements
Every escalation note must cite the specific policy section identifiers, in the form used throughout this document (for example, MRLP-6.1), that support its root-cause classification and recommended action, and, where the note references a regulatory standard, the specific Basel Framework paragraph identifier (for example, a MAR33 paragraph) rather than a general reference to "Basel" or "the market risk standard." A citation to a section or paragraph that does not exist, or that was not actually consulted in preparing the note, is not permitted; citations must reflect material genuinely retrieved and used in the analysis. This requirement supports the deterministic citation check applied before a note is approved, which confirms that every cited identifier exists in the underlying corpus. A note should cite the most specific section supporting each claim — for example MRLP-6.6 for a data-quality routing decision, not the general MRLP-6 — so a reviewer can verify the claim against a narrow, checkable piece of text.

### MRLP-7.4 Tone and prioritization
An escalation note is written for a risk manager who needs to act quickly and correctly, not for a general audience. The note must lead with the recommended action and the key facts of the breach, before presenting supporting detail; a risk manager should be able to understand what happened and what is being proposed from the first two or three sentences. The note must be concise: it should include enough evidence to satisfy MRLP-7.2 and MRLP-7.3 without restating background information the reader is assumed to already know, such as the general structure of the Bank's limit framework. Speculative language, hedged conclusions without a clear recommendation, or marketing-style framing have no place in an escalation note; the tone throughout should be direct, factual, and oriented toward the decision the risk manager must make.

## MRLP-8 Approval and Dispatch

### MRLP-8.1 Human approval required
No escalation note may be dispatched to its intended recipients without explicit human approval from a risk manager on duty. This requirement applies regardless of how the note was drafted, including where a note was drafted with the assistance of an automated investigation process; automation may prepare a note, but it may never send one. The approval step is a hard interrupt in the Bank's incident-handling process: the process pauses and persists its state at this point and does not proceed to dispatch until an approval decision is recorded. This requirement exists because an escalation note, however well evidenced, represents a judgment about the cause and appropriate response to a risk event, and that judgment must be exercised by an accountable individual before it is communicated to a desk head, the CRO, or the Board Risk Committee.

### MRLP-8.2 Approver may edit the note
The risk manager on duty reviewing a note for approval is not limited to a binary approve-or-reject decision. The approver may edit the note's content, including its wording, its recommended action, or the evidence presented, before approving it for dispatch. Edits made at this stage become part of the final note that is sent and are recorded as part of the approval record described in MRLP-8.3. This flexibility recognizes that a drafted note, even one meeting the evidence and citation standards in MRLP-7, may benefit from a human reviewer's judgment about phrasing, additional context specific to the day's events, or a different emphasis than the draft proposed, without requiring the entire note to be rejected and redrafted from scratch. Any edit that changes the note's stated root cause, recommended action, or a cited figure must still satisfy the evidence and citation standards in MRLP-7.2 and MRLP-7.3; the approver's discretion to edit does not relax those standards.

### MRLP-8.3 Approval record
Every approval decision is recorded, including the identity of the approving risk manager, the timestamp of the decision, whether the note was approved as drafted or edited before approval, and, for an approved note, a server-issued approval token that authorizes dispatch. This token is a control mechanism: dispatch systems require a valid, unused approval token before sending any note, which prevents a note from being sent without a recorded approval decision, even in the event of a process error elsewhere in the system. The approval record, together with the final text of the note as sent, is retained as part of the incident record required under MRLP-4.5 and is available for audit. A note may be dispatched only once against a given approval token; a token is not reusable for a separately drafted note, even for the same underlying breach, and any resend requires a fresh approval decision.

### MRLP-8.4 Rejected notes
A risk manager on duty may reject an escalation note rather than approve it, with or without edits. A rejected note is closed with a documented reason, which may include that the evidence does not support the stated root cause, that the recommended action is inconsistent with the routing table in MRLP-6.7, that further investigation is needed before the note can be finalized, or that the underlying breach itself requires re-validation under MRLP-4.4. A rejected note does not proceed to dispatch under any circumstance. Depending on the reason for rejection, the incident may be returned to Market Risk Management for further investigation and a revised note, or, where the rejection reveals that the underlying alert was not a true breach, closed under MRLP-5.4.

## MRLP-9 Temporary Limit Increases and Exceptions

### MRLP-9.1 Authority levels
Temporary increases to a desk-level Value-at-Risk limit may be approved by the Chief Risk Officer on the recommendation of Market Risk Management and the relevant desk head. Temporary increases to the firm-level Value-at-Risk limit or to the firm-level stress-loss limit require Chief Risk Officer approval and notification to the Board Risk Committee, given the more significant governance interest in firm-wide risk levels described in MRLP-2.1. No desk head, risk manager on duty, or member of Market Risk Management below the CRO has authority to approve a temporary limit increase at any level; their role is confined to recommending one, with supporting rationale, as part of the remediation options described in MRLP-6.4. A request for a temporary increase should specify the scope and metric affected, the size requested, and the expected duration, so the approving authority can weigh it against the Bank's risk appetite rather than approve an open-ended change to MRLP-3.

### MRLP-9.2 Duration and conditions
A temporary limit increase is granted for a fixed, stated duration, agreed at the time of approval, and does not become a permanent revision to the limit table in MRLP-3.2 or MRLP-3.3. At the expiry of the agreed duration, the desk or firm must be back within the original limit, or the increase must be explicitly renewed through the same approval process; an increase does not automatically extend. A temporary increase may be granted subject to conditions, such as a requirement that the position driving the breach be hedged within the increase period, or that no further increase to that position be taken while the temporary limit is in effect. Conditions attached to an increase are enforced by Market Risk Management as part of its ongoing daily monitoring.

### MRLP-9.3 Documentation
Every temporary limit increase is documented at the time of approval, recording the original limit, the temporary limit, the duration, the rationale, any conditions attached, and the approving authority under MRLP-9.1. This documentation is retained alongside the incident record for the breach that prompted the increase, so that the connection between the breach, its root-cause classification, and the resulting exception is auditable. Expiry or renewal of a temporary increase is likewise documented, including confirmation of whether the desk or firm returned within the original limit at expiry. Undocumented or verbal exceptions to a limit are not permitted under this policy. The documentation for a temporary increase follows the same result-identifier discipline required of an escalation note under MRLP-7.2, so a subsequent reviewer, including the Board Risk Committee, can trace the exception back to the breach that prompted it.

## MRLP-10 Backtesting and Model Linkage

### MRLP-10.1 Daily backtesting
The Bank backtests its Value-at-Risk model daily by comparing the prior day's 1-day 99% Value-at-Risk against that day's hypothetical profit and loss — the profit or loss that the prior day's static positions would have realized under the actual market move, holding positions fixed. This hypothetical-P&L approach isolates the performance of the Value-at-Risk model itself from the effect of intraday trading. An exception is recorded whenever the realized loss exceeds the prior day's Value-at-Risk figure. Exceptions are counted over a rolling 250-day window, consistent with the Basel Framework's backtesting expectations under MAR32, and are reviewed together with statistical independence and coverage tests to confirm that exceptions are not clustering in a way that would indicate a systematic model weakness rather than expected statistical variation.

### MRLP-10.2 Basel traffic-light zones
Backtesting exceptions over the trailing 250-day window are classified into traffic-light zones consistent with the Basel Framework: green for 0 to 4 exceptions, yellow for 5 to 9 exceptions, and red for 10 or more exceptions. A green zone result indicates the model's performance is consistent with its stated confidence level and requires no action. A yellow zone result requires Market Risk Management to investigate whether the exception count reflects a genuine model weakness, an unusual run of market conditions, or a data issue, and to report its findings to the CRO. A red zone result is a serious finding that requires the CRO to consider a model review, and until that review is complete, the limit calibration in MRLP-3.5 should be treated as provisionally less reliable, since the model producing the underlying Value-at-Risk figures may be understating risk.

### MRLP-10.3 Limit review after a model change
Any material change to the Value-at-Risk model — including changes to the historical simulation window, the volatility or covariance methodology, or the scope of risk factors covered — triggers a mandatory review of the limits in MRLP-3.2 and MRLP-3.3, since a model change can shift the distribution of the metric being measured against a limit calibrated under the prior model. This review follows the same calibration methodology described in MRLP-3.5, but is performed out of the annual cycle, on the calibration window applicable at the time of the model change. Limits are not automatically carried forward unchanged after a model change; Market Risk Management must explicitly confirm, and the Board Risk Committee approve, that the existing limits remain appropriate, or approve revised limits, before the new model is used for live limit monitoring.

## MRLP-11 Governance

### MRLP-11.1 Policy owner and approval
This policy is owned by Market Risk Management and is approved by the Chief Risk Officer, on behalf of the Board Risk Committee, before it takes effect. The Head of Market Risk Management is accountable for keeping the policy current, for proposing revisions when the limit framework, escalation paths, or roles described in it change, and for ensuring that the policy is consistent with the Market Data Controls Procedure and with the Bank's broader risk governance framework. Any revision to this policy, including a change to a limit value, a threshold, or an escalation path, must go through the same approval chain as the original policy before it is treated as effective. Draft revisions may be prepared by any member of Market Risk Management, but only the Head of Market Risk Management may submit one for Chief Risk Officer approval, and no revision takes effect until reflected in the version history in MRLP-11.3.

### MRLP-11.2 Review cycle
This policy is reviewed at least annually, in conjunction with the limit calibration exercise described in MRLP-3.5, and is reviewed out of cycle whenever a material change occurs to the Bank's book, its risk model, or its risk appetite as set by the Board Risk Committee. The annual review confirms that the roles and responsibilities in MRLP-2, the limit framework in MRLP-3, and the escalation paths in MRLP-6 remain fit for purpose, and incorporates any lessons learned from breaches, near misses, or rejected escalation notes recorded over the prior year. Findings from the annual review that do not require an immediate policy change are recorded in the Bank's governance minutes rather than in this document. Between scheduled reviews, the Head of Market Risk Management may flag an emerging concern — such as rising warning-band observations under MRLP-4.3 or a cluster of rejected notes under MRLP-8.4 — for early Board Risk Committee attention.
